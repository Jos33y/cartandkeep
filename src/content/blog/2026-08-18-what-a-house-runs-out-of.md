There is a kind of shopping nobody enjoys. The bin bags run out on a Sunday, the bathroom scale stops working, a birthday arrives a day sooner than it should. None of it is interesting, all of it costs money, and most of it gets bought in a hurry at whatever price is in front of you.

This is a short guide to the things that go first in a UK home, and how to buy them calmly instead.

## The ones that run out without warning

**Cleaning and drain treatments.** Nobody buys these until the water is standing still in the sink. Buying one before you need it costs the same and saves an evening.

**Batteries and adapters.** The remote, the smoke alarm, the thing in the loft. Singles bought in an emergency cost more than one pack that lives in a drawer.

**Bin liners.** The only real decision is size. Measuring the bin once is cheaper than buying the wrong size twice.

## The ones that quietly wear out

**Bathroom scales.** A well made mechanical scale can last for years. A cheap digital one often does not, which is worse value once you do the arithmetic.

**Filters and refills.** Most people keep using a filter well past its date. The refill is the real product; the jug or the unit is the thing you buy once.

**Cookware.** The clearest example of price against cost. A cheap set replaced every couple of years usually costs more than a better one that lasts, and it is considerably more annoying.

## The ones people forget until the day

**Gifts.** Last minute gifts are bought at the highest price available. One good gift set kept in a cupboard removes an entire category of panic.

**Storage.** Clear boxes, not opaque ones. Opaque boxes become a place things go to be forgotten.

## The pattern

Everything here has the same shape. It is bought urgently, at whatever price is available, from whoever happens to have it. That is how a household ends up spending more than it needs to on things that were never really a choice.

The alternative is not clever.

> It is having the dull things in before you need them.
