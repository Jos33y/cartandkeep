There is a particular kind of shopping nobody enjoys. The tap starts dripping, or the bin bags run out on a Sunday, or somebody notices the bathroom scale has been broken since March. None of it is interesting. All of it costs money, and most of it gets bought in a hurry at whatever price is in front of you.

This is a list of the things that go first in a UK home, what a sensible price looks like for each, and where people usually overpay.

## The ones that go without warning

**Drain and sink treatments.** Nobody buys these until the water is standing still. A litre of a decent outdoor cleaner or a 500ml drain treatment runs around £17 to £19, and the reason it feels expensive in the moment is that you are buying it under pressure. Buying one before you need it costs the same and saves the evening.

**Batteries and adapters.** The remote, the smoke alarm, the thing in the loft. A multipack of connectors or a socket adapter sits around £18, and the trap here is buying singles: three separate emergency purchases cost more than one pack that lives in a drawer.

**Bin liners.** Around £17 for a bulk pack, and the only real decision is size. Buying the wrong size twice costs more than measuring the bin once.

## The ones that quietly wear out

**Bathroom scales.** A mechanical scale is about £25 and lasts a decade. A cheap digital one is £12 and lasts two years, which is worse value the moment you do the arithmetic.

**Water filter cartridges.** Roughly £35 for a six pack, which is where most people wince and then keep using a filter three months past its date. The cartridges are the product; the jug is the thing you buy once.

**Pans.** A five piece non stick set is around £117, and it is the clearest example on this list of the difference between price and cost. A £30 set replaced every two years is more expensive than a £117 set that lasts eight, and considerably more annoying.

**Floor lamps.** A corner or tree lamp is £32 to £48. Lighting is the cheapest way to change how a room feels, and it is almost always the last thing anybody thinks of.

## The ones people forget until the day

**Gift sets.** A pamper hamper is £37 to £43 and the entire market runs on people buying at the last minute at the highest available price. Keeping one in a cupboard costs nothing extra and removes an entire category of panic.

**Storage boxes.** A three pack of 35 litre clear boxes is around £47. The mistake is buying opaque ones, which turn into a place things go to be forgotten.

## The ones with a pet in the house

**Bedding and beds.** A raised cooling dog bed is around £44 and a 10kg bale of paper bedding is around £50. Both last, and both get bought reactively after a mess.

**Calming refills.** Around £53 for a three month pack. Not cheap, and the households that use them do not consider it optional.

## The pattern

Everything on this list has the same shape. It is bought urgently, at whatever price is available, from whoever happens to have it. That is how a household ends up spending noticeably more than it needs to on things that were never a choice in the first place.

The alternative is not clever. It is a cupboard with the boring things already in it.
