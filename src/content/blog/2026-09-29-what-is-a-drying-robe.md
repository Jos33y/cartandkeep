A drying robe is a long, loose, hooded robe with a warm lining, worn to get dry, get changed and stay warm outdoors. You put it on over wet swimwear or damp kit, change underneath it in privacy, and keep it on until you are warm again. If you spend time outside in the cold and wet, it is one of the most useful things you can own.

## Who actually uses a drying robe?

They started with surfers and sea swimmers, but most people who buy one now are not athletes at all:

- **Open water and sea swimmers**, getting changed on a beach or lakeside.
- **Surfers and paddleboarders**, between sessions and after.
- **Parents** standing at the side of a pitch, a pool or a cold touchline.
- **Dog walkers and campers**, for early mornings and wet evenings.
- **Anyone who hates getting cold** after a swim, a run or a match.

## What makes a drying robe different from a dressing gown?

Three things:

1. **Room to change inside it.** A drying robe is cut wide enough to take your arms inside and change underneath without anyone seeing.
2. **A lining that dries or warms you.** Towelling linings dry you off. Fleece or sherpa linings keep you warm.
3. **An outer made for outdoors.** Some have a waterproof shell. Others have a softer outer for warmth and comfort rather than rain.

## What should you look for when buying one?

- **Size.** Go roomy. If you cannot pull your arms inside to change, it is too small.
- **Lining.** Towelling if drying off matters most, fleece if staying warm does.
- **Hood.** A generous hood keeps your head warm and gives more privacy when changing.
- **Zip.** A two way zip lets you open the bottom to walk or sit without undoing it all.
- **Pockets.** Deep pockets for keys, a phone and cold hands.
- **Length.** Past the knee keeps more of you out of the wind.

## Is a drying robe worth the money?

If you are regularly outdoors when it is cold or wet, yes. It replaces a towel, a coat and a changing tent in one, and it goes straight on in seconds. If you only swim indoors, a good towel and a warm jumper will do the same job.

## How do you wash and look after a drying robe?

- Rinse off sea water and sand as soon as you can. Salt and grit wear the fabric.
- Wash on a cool cycle, following the care label.
- Dry it fully before you fold it away. A damp robe stored in a bag is how mildew starts.
- Skip fabric conditioner on towelling linings, as it makes them less absorbent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you get changed inside a drying robe?

Yes, that is what it is designed for. The cut is wide enough to take your arms inside and change underneath it.

### Are drying robes waterproof?

Some are and some are not. Robes with a waterproof shell keep rain off. Others use a softer outer for warmth. Check the product details if rain matters to you.

### What size drying robe should I get?

Size up rather than down. You need room to move your arms inside it and to wear it over layers.

### Is a drying robe the same as a changing robe?

Yes. Drying robe, changing robe and swim robe are all names for the same kind of garment.
