For most homes, a good mechanical bathroom scale is the better buy. It needs no batteries, the dial is easy to read, and a well made one lasts for years. A digital scale is worth it if you want readings to a tenth of a kilo, body composition estimates, or tracking on your phone.

Here is how to decide, and how to get a reading you can actually trust from either.

## What is the difference between a mechanical and a digital scale?

A **mechanical scale** uses springs and a dial. When you stand on it, the springs compress and turn the dial to your weight. There is nothing to charge and nothing to go flat.

A **digital scale** uses electronic sensors and shows your weight on a screen. Most read to the nearest 100 grams. Some estimate body fat, water and muscle, and some connect to an app.

## Which is more accurate?

Both can be accurate. What matters more is how you use them. A scale on carpet, or one moved around the house, will give different numbers whatever kind it is. The fairest comparison is this:

- **Digital** shows smaller steps, so it looks more precise. That also means it shows every glass of water and every meal.
- **Mechanical** shows broader steps, so it smooths out the day to day noise and makes the trend easier to see.

If you want to know whether your weight is going up, down or staying level, either works. If you need to track small changes precisely, choose digital.

## Who should choose a mechanical scale?

- Anyone who finds small screens or buttons hard to use.
- Older relatives, where a large dial and no batteries mean fewer things to go wrong.
- Households that just want a steady weekly check, without an app.

## Who should choose a digital scale?

- People following a plan that needs precise numbers.
- Anyone who wants their readings logged automatically.
- People curious about body composition, understanding that these figures are estimates rather than medical measurements.

One caution: body composition scales pass a small electrical current through the body. Most manufacturers advise against using them if you have a pacemaker or other implanted medical device, and during pregnancy. Check the instructions.

## How do you get a reading you can trust?

1. **Put the scale on a hard, level floor.** Carpet and rugs are the most common reason for odd readings.
2. **Leave it in one place.** Moving it between rooms can change the reading.
3. **Weigh at the same time of day.** First thing in the morning, before eating, is the most consistent.
4. **Set the zero.** On a mechanical scale, check the dial sits exactly on zero before you step on, and use the adjuster wheel if it does not.
5. **Look at the trend, not the day.** Weekly readings tell you far more than daily ones.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are mechanical scales accurate?

A good mechanical scale is accurate enough for tracking your weight over time, especially when it stays on a hard floor and is zeroed before use. Digital scales show smaller steps, which is not the same as being more useful.

### Why does my scale show a different weight each time?

Usually because of the floor, the position of your feet, or moving the scale. Stand in the same place on it, on a hard floor, at the same time of day.

### Should a bathroom scale go on carpet?

No. Carpet lets the scale tilt and sink, which throws the reading out. Use a hard, level floor.

### Can you get scales that show stones and kilograms?

Yes. Many scales sold in the UK show both, either on the dial or with a switch on a digital model. Check the product details before buying.
