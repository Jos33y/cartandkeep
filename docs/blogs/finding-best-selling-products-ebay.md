**How to Validate If a Product Will Actually Sell**
---------------------------------------------------

!eBay's Sold Items filter to find best selling products

Finding a product is easy. Validating it is where most sellers fail. Before listing anything, you want clear signals that the product has real demand, manageable competition, and a reliable supply behind it.

Here are a few tricks to do it right:

*   **Sales velocity indicators.** This is the first thing to check. It tells you how fast the product is selling. Look at how many units have been sold recently and how often listings are converting. If you see consistent daily or weekly sales across multiple sellers, that’s a strong sign of real demand.

> **🔍 Research Tip:** Use eBay’s _“Sold items”_ filter and look for products with consistent recent sales across multiple sellers. Then, for each listing, check for the “Items sold” data. The more, the better, as it confirms real demand.

!eBay's units sold data to find top selling items

*   **Competition saturation signals.** High demand doesn’t always mean opportunity. If there are hundreds of identical listings competing, it’s likely saturated. Watch for signals like aggressive undercutting, identical product images, and overcrowded search results. Ideally, you want demand that isn’t fully dominated yet.
*   **Pricing consistency.** Stable pricing is a good sign. Are most sellers offering the product within a similar price range? Good. It usually means the market has found a balance and there’s still room for profit. On the other hand, if prices are constantly dropping, it’s not really worth it. It means competition will eat your margins. 
*   **Supplier reliability check.** Even if demand looks great, unreliable suppliers can ruin the opportunity. Make sure the product is consistently in stock, shipping times are reasonable, and order fulfillment is smooth. Trusted suppliers reduce delays, cancellations, and unhappy customers.

**What to Avoid When Choosing Products**
----------------------------------------

As we’ve already mentioned, finding the right eBay product is not about randomly picking whatever you like. There are methods and strategies that show you the way. Likewise, there are things to avoid, such as:

*   **Oversaturated products.** If everyone is selling the exact item with the same images and titles, welcome to a price war. Oversaturated products usually lead to lower margins and constant undercutting. A good rule of thumb: if you can’t clearly differentiate your listing, it’s probably too crowded. 
*   **Copyright / branded items.** Selling branded or copyrighted products without authorization is risky. Platforms like eBay are strict about intellectual property, and violations can lead to listing removals or even account issues. Stick to generic or unbranded products (unless you’re working with approved suppliers) and **avoid VeRO violations**.
*   **Low-margin products.** This comes down to one thing: why bother? If it doesn’t leave enough room for profit after factoring in product cost, fees, and shipping, it’s not worth the effort. 
*   **Products with long shipping times.** Customers don’t choose products that take 15-25 days to arrive. Long delivery times hurt conversions and lead to negative feedback. Always prioritize suppliers that offer reliable and fast shipping to keep your store competitive. 

**Common Product Research Mistakes**
------------------------------------

Mistakes can happen. But if you know what to look out for ahead of time, it’s easy to avoid them. 

Let’s start with the most common one: **relying on trends.** You see a fidget spinner going viral on TikTok, and _puff_: added to catalog. But here’s the thing: trends are useful, but not enough. A product going viral doesn’t mean it will sell. Trends are a starting point, but then you need to check real demand and sales data.

Another common mistake? **Ignoring competition.** Strong demand is good, but if everyone and your grandma are selling it, it becomes much harder to stand out. Ignoring competition = pricing wars and low margins.

Same with **copying listings without validation**. It’s tempting to copy and paste what’s already working, but it can backfire. Just because a listing looks successful doesn’t mean it still is. Always check recent sales, pricing trends, and supplier costs before replicating.

Lastly, **not considering fulfillment**. Long shipping times, unreliable suppliers, or stock issues can lead to issues. And, honestly? No product is worth the headache. Always think beyond the listing.
