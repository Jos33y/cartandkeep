**What Is eBay Dropshipping?**
------------------------------

To **start a dropshipping business**, first, we must choose a selling channel. Selling channels are **marketplaces and eCommerce websites** where we can **market and sell** products. In fact, if we use a **marketplace like eBay,** we can leverage the trust and recognition that the marketplace already has. On top of that, we can utilize the **large audience** that is readily available on eBay.

To clarify though, dropshipping is an online **retail fulfillment method** where the seller does not keep the inventory they sell in stock. Rather, we purchase the product from a third-party supplier when we make a sale. The supplier then directly ships the product to our customer. Because of this, the dropshipping model is **low-risk and low-investment** with **high-profit potential**.

When it comes to the **cost of eBay dropshipping**, it can vary depending on several factors. Here are some **factors to consider** when dropshipping on eBay:

### **Free Listings**

eBay is a great choice to start dropshipping since it allows us to have **250 free listings** to begin with. That way, we can keep our initial investments nonexistent or down to a minimum. However, **if we want more listings**, we need to pay per listing, or, more conveniently, we can pay for a **subscription**. ### **Subscription Fees**

eBay offers various subscription plans designed to cater to different levels of sellers, each with its own set of fees and benefits. The main plans are Starter, Basic, Premium, Anchor, and Enterprise.

The **Starter plan** is ideal for beginners, offering low-cost access with limited listings. The **Basic plan** provides a higher number of free listings and lower final value fees, making it suitable for growing businesses. The **Premium plan** offers even more listings and benefits like discounts on eBay-branded shipping supplies. The **Anchor plan** is designed for high-volume sellers, with dedicated customer support and even greater listing allowances. Lastly, the **Enterprise plan** caters to large-scale businesses with custom solutions, offering the highest levels of support and listings.

Each plan comes with its own monthly or annual subscription fee, which increases with the plan’s features and support options, allowing sellers to choose one that best fits their needs.

### **Final Value Fees**

After insertion fees, we have to consider **final value fees**. Final value fees are fees charged by eBay for selling items on their platform. The fee is calculated as a percentage of the total sale amount (item price + postage and handling charges) plus a fixed charge of $0.30 per order. 

The final value fee **can change** based on the product category sold. For instance, let’s assume we have subscribed to a **starter store** selling men’s shoes. As such, we have to pay **8% per item** if the sale amount is over $150 and 12.9% per item if the total sales are under $150.

On the other hand, if we have Basic, Premium, Anchor, and Enterprise store subscriptions, we have to pay **7% per item** if the sale amount is over $150 and 12% per item if the total sales are under $150. The final value fee is calculated and automatically charged by eBay at the end of a sale through **eBay Managed Payments**. 

### **Promotional Fees**

Standing out from the crowd is crucial when dropshipping on eBay. So, besides the mandatory fees, we might generate promotional fees. These are fees we pay eBay to market our products. Without a solid marketing strategy, getting our products in front of the right people and making sales can be difficult. 

As such, eBay also charges a **promotional fee** for sellers who choose to use the platform’s promoted listing features, Promoted Listings Standard or Promoted Listings Advanced.

When we utilize the promoted listings standard, we pay eBay a set percentage only after we make a sale from their promo. On the other hand, with promoted listings advanced, we pay a daily budget in advance to run our ads. This works similarly to the way pay-per-click (PPC) ads work.

### **10\. Dealing With ‘Flagged’ Accounts**

First and foremost, we need to understand what a **flagged eBay account** is. It means that eBay listings of flagged dropshipping stores will be displayed lower in the search results of eBay, and they will have fewer sales. 

Now, it’s essential to understand that our eBay account can’t be unflagged. But, we can learn how to work with a flagged account and still make our dropshipping business profitable. The way to deal with a flagged account and still see lots of sales is by listing many products. 

When our account gets flagged, we’ll notice a small decrease in traffic coming to our store. However, when we list more products, it doesn’t really matter since we increase our overall store traffic, which means more sales. This strategy is the **big numbers rule** where we upload products in bulk in our eBay dropshipping stores and start optimizing from there.

Alternatively, to avoid being flagged, we need to offer our customers an awesome shopping experience. To do this, we need: 

*   Great customer service
*   Low returns rate
*   Good shipping time 
*   Competitive prices

> Do not confuse “flagged” with **eBay MC011 restricted accounts**. These are not the same.
