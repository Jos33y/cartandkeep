## What I would do differently

### The big one

**Report the product's answer, not the route's answer.** Four times today a
product that passed every gate was described as failing, because the supply
route I happened to test could not work at its volume. The cushioned raised
toilet seat has £9.10 of headroom, one seller, and seven windows of history. It
is a good product. What failed was importing 200 units of an eleven a month
product from Wenzhou. Those are different sentences and I said the wrong one.

**Every product has two numbers and I read them as one.** Headroom per unit
says what it can pay for goods. Monthly worth says whether it repays a stock
buy. £9.10 and £100 a month are both true and they point to different suppliers.

**Match the route to how she trades.** She buys single units and sells single
units. Container arithmetic was never the right test and I ran it four times
before he stopped me.

### Screens and word lists

**A blanket exclusion usually contains its own release condition.** The
electrical screen existed because compliance sits with the importer. That binds
whoever imports, not whoever sells, so a UK trade supply route removes it
entirely. The document said so and nobody had read the sentence.

**Test in both directions on real titles, and expect a third of your
expectations to be wrong.** Sixteen titles disagreed with the code on the first
corpus run. Nine were my expectations, seven were real gaps.

**Audience words are not identity words.** `elderly`, `disabled`, `mobility`,
`adult` separated five listings of one product, because rarity weighting treats
a rare word as an informative one and those are the least informative words in
the title.

**Three letter words carry products.** `lid`, `bar`, `mat`, `pad`, `tog`. The
identity matcher dropped every word under four characters.

### Numbers

**A per unit ceiling does not add across products.** £339.48 of "headroom still
to decide" was 41 ceilings summed, which nobody can earn. Units times headroom
is the figure that means something.

**A wholesale price is not a landed cost.** Factory £12.73 became £20.41 by the
time duty, freight and unreclaimable import VAT were added. The portal shows a
goods field and will mislead in exactly this way when a quote arrives.

**Read the price matrix, not the headline price.** £10.03 was white single.
Aubergine single was £15.91. The whole conclusion turned on that cell.

**Terapeak's average blends every variation.** A listing at £24.21 across 72
variations sells its single at £19.99. Any row with a high variation count
carries this distortion.

### Method

**A term dies in one page load, and that is the method working.** `disability
aids` and `orthopaedic support` both closed inside twenty minutes. Do not treat
that as a setback.

**But four terms in three hours is thrashing.** Two categories were already on
screen undecided. More candidates were not the missing thing.

## Recurring failure modes to watch

1. **Testing the route and reporting the product.** New this session and it
   happened four times in one afternoon.
2. **Asserting a rate, a page or a price instead of reading it.**
3. **Building before verifying.**
4. **Optimism in defaults.** A null must never become a zero.
5. **Printing a verdict the data cannot support.**
6. **Generalising from one row, one price, or one cell of a matrix.**
7. **Fixing a fault on one page and not the others that share it.**
8. **Sweeping another term when the last two are still undecided.**
9. **Continuing past a negative result.**
10. **Correct and lifeless design.**
