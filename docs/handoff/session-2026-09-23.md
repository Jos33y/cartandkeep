## The collector was broken and is now fixed

Home Bargains had been giving 642 products from 6 departments. Three probes
established why, and none of it was guesswork:

- The products are in the page data, not the rendered grid, twenty a page, with
  a total page count beside them.
- The pager is a `div` carrying `data-value`, not a link, so no button matcher
  would ever have found it.
- Paging is `?sortby=newest&currentpage=2&perpage=40`, all lowercase. Every
  other parameter name returns page one, which is how a six page category read
  as twenty products.

Rewritten to read the page data and page by URL. **3,836 products across the
whole shop, and 4,460 with seasonal.** Prices are pence in the source, so a
£4.99 oil was about to be read as £499.

Three fields came with it that we had been asking for: `pricePerMetric`, which
settles a pack size argument at collection time; `maxSaleQty`, which says what
the shop will sell one person; and the shop's own brand tag.

## What the gate refuses now

**Entry proved became a pass condition.** Until today, "the smallest recent
seller has 120,471 feedback" was a note on a card that still reached her. It
now ends the candidate. Applied to what we had already gated, that sends 3
products instead of 22.

It splits three ways rather than two, which matters: only large accounts
selling and a small seller barely selling both fail, and no recent listing at
all fails only in a crowded market. A thin market with no evidence either way
is still worth a try, which is what keeps replacement parts in.

**The median now comes from listings that are selling**, not from the field.
On one sample the listed median was £26.50 and the selling median £29.99,
moving the buying ceiling from £16.69 to £19.63.

**A buying window, and a band check.** Goods under £3 or over £40 fail, and a
market priced above £80 on the default Evri band is refused as "not a parcel"
rather than costed at £2.62. That is what stopped a lounge chair passing with a
£558 ceiling.

**New and fixed price only**, in both the scan and the gate. Used goods price
higher than new on appliances, so an unfiltered median reads as room to buy
that nobody selling new stock has.

## The £10 floor, and what it cost

The goods floor was raised to £10 yesterday, reasoning from the mining route
where a ceiling arrives with no supplier behind it. On a shelf run the price is
known first, so a cheap product in a dearer market is exactly the shape worth
having, and £10 refused those.

Lowered to £3, and the £2 a sale target left to decide per product. Re-judging
the scans recovered about 300 phrases and the gate re-judge recovered five
products, including Nature Valley bars where a 257 feedback seller has sold 18
in 46 days.

Both the scan and the gate now re-judge from what they already measured, so a
threshold change never costs a call again.
