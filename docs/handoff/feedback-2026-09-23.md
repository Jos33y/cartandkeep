# Feedback, 23 September 2026

**Cart & Keep** | Internal

What went wrong in how I worked today, so the next session does not repeat it.

---

## Cutting on category instead of letting the gate decide

The worst habit of the day. I wrote skip lists that removed laundry, shampoo,
nappies, dog treats, gift sets and biscuit tins before a single call was spent,
on the reasoning that supermarkets own those markets.

Then Dentastix, a dog treat, turned out to have a 24 feedback seller moving 22
boxes in 18 days. The Flexitol heel balm, a chemist line, is the strongest
sales rate we have seen. Both would have been cut by my own list if it had been
applied to the band they came from.

You pushed back twice: once on costumes and wigs, once on "let's not just cut
anything anyhow". Both times you were right and the reasoning was the same: the
gate refuses on measured facts, and a category cut is an assumption dressed as
efficiency.

The rule I should have started with: only cut what the gate cannot see. Account
risk and sizing qualify. What kind of product it is does not.

---

## Reasoning from the old route

The £10 goods floor was correct for mined phrases, where a ceiling arrives with
no supplier behind it. It was wrong the moment the route changed to shelf
first, and I carried it across without re-deriving it. It refused about 300
phrases and five products for a day.

The same mistake in miniature: I said cheap Christmas decorations could not
work, when the floor is on the market's ceiling, not on the product's price. A
£4 bauble in a £20 market is exactly the shape worth having.

When a route changes, every threshold set under the old one needs re-deriving,
not inheriting.

---

## Not failing loudly enough

The scan ran 700 calls into a rate limit, wrote every failure into a CSV, and
that CSV then counted as a completed slice. Three separate faults in one: no
stop on 429, no refusal to write a file of errors, and a resume check that only
skipped a file where every row had failed.

A tool that keeps going after the thing it depends on has stopped answering is
worse than one that crashes, because it produces something that looks like
data.

---

## Quoting a figure I had not checked

I read the alarm clock's shelf price as £12.99 and told you it lost money. It
is £9.99, the importer was right, and I was arguing against a correct tool from
memory of an earlier output.

This is the same error the research rules have carried since August: verify
before quoting. The figure was on screen and I did not look.

---

## Guessing at shapes

I wrote a price reader for the Home Bargains data before seeing one whole
product, and it would have read £4.99 as £499 because the source is in pence.
The `--sample` flag exists now for exactly that reason, and it should have been
the first thing written, not the second.

Same pattern with the pagination: three probes, because the first two were
written against what I expected rather than what the page contained.

---

## What went well, for balance

The probes themselves were the right move once started. Each one answered a
specific question and the collector was then written against evidence rather
than assumption, which is why it worked first time.

Refusing to write the gate's bulk-read optimisation on a guess, and probing the
API instead, saved a rewrite: the endpoint returns 403 and the saving does not
exist.

And the day ended with the pipeline, the migrations and twelve new modules in
version control for the first time, which matters more than any of the
research.
