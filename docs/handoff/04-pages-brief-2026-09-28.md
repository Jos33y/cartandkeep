## Why these pages, and the bar

The buy path now has a visual language of its own: the parcel label on kraft board, the three stops, the dashed reference ticket, the round postmark, pinned photographs, stamps. The pages below were built before that language existed and read as text pages next to it.

Joseph's words on the work so far set the bar: "very lazy and no creativity no icons", "no visual uplift", then, once it landed, "wonderful and creative progress". These pages should go further than the buy path, not match it. Each one should have one idea a person remembers, taken from what a general store and a parcel actually are, and never a template: no stock hero, no three-column feature grid, no generic icons in circles.

Premium and calm still holds. The idea carries the page; motion and ornament serve it or go.

## In scope

In this order. Current state is from the code in the collected copy of the repo; confirm against the real tree at the start.

### 1. About (`pages/store/AboutPage.jsx`, `data/about.js`)

- Now: breadcrumb, heading "A general store, not a niche.", text blocks from `data/about.js`, a "What we will not do" list whose items strike through as they scroll in, and an "At a glance" facts card (who runs it, based at, delivery, returns, paying).
- The strike-through is the one real idea on the page. Keep or better it.
- "Who runs it" stays hidden until she gives her own two or three sentences (`herWords` is null). Do not write them for her.
- The words in `data/about.js` are true of the shop today and mostly good. The job is the page, not a rewrite.

### 2. Contact (`pages/store/ContactPage.jsx`)

- Now: the address as copyable text (a bare mailto opens nothing on many laptops), prefilled subjects for the three things people write about, and the trader identity distance selling requires.
- No form, on purpose: there is nothing behind one yet, and a form that loses a message is worse than an address. Keep that unless a real route exists.

### 3. Writing (`pages/store/BlogPage.jsx`, `BlogPostPage.jsx`, `data/blog.js`)

- Posts are markdown files in `src/content/blog/` read at build time. Confirm how many exist; the collected copy did not include the folder.
- Both the list and the post page need designing: what a post looks like on the list, how a post reads, what sits after it.

### 4. Request (`pages/store/RequestPage.jsx`)

- The request slip: one required field, the rest optional. Emails her through the `request` function, which now uses the shared email templates.
- The home page already calls it a request slip. The page could be one.

### 5. Unsubscribe (`pages/store/UnsubscribePage.jsx`)

- Runs on load, no "are you sure". Small page, but it is the last thing some people see of the shop.

### Maybe

- Help (`pages/store/FaqPage.jsx`) was rebuilt as a help desk on 25 September. It works, but it has not had the new visual treatment. Joseph to say whether it is in.

## Material to draw from

These are starting points to propose from, not decisions.

- The shop's own lines, left exactly as she wrote them: "Shop it! Love it! Keep it!" and "A small UK shop with a very large cupboard" (top bar).
- The parcel language already built: kraft board and paper label, outward postcode large, the gift stamp, the round PAID postmark with franking lines, the dashed ticket, the three stops.
- A general store as a physical thing: shelves, a cupboard, a counter, a shop sign, price tickets, a notebook of requests.
- The refusals list on About: the page's argument, made visible.
- Real photographs of her products are available through the catalogue for anything that needs them.

## How the work runs

1. Read each page, its CSS module and its data file before proposing.
2. Propose per page: the one idea, what the page shows top to bottom, what it reuses, what is new. Wait for a yes.
3. Build into the real components with tokens and shared primitives, never standalone mocks.
4. Test at all 13 screen sizes for overflow, run the page suite and the build. Joseph screenshots the live build.
5. Deliver a zip of only the files changed that round.

Style: British English, no em or en dashes anywhere, no AI-tell words, customer copy says "you". Her own wording is never edited.
