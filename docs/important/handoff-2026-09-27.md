## The daily routine

1. `node scripts/limits.js` for calls left.
2. `node scripts/ledger.mjs` shows every query file: scanned, part done (with the `--start=` to resume), or untouched. Writes `research/ledger.md`.
3. For each slice: **scan, gate, import.** Never scan more than can be gated the same day, unless scanning ahead at night on spare calls (scans stay valid a day).
4. Rotation: every source gets a slice a day, about 250 phrases. Care has paid best (about 16 to 22 cards per 1,000 calls), then General shops and Hannworld (about 20), Toolstation and CPC weaker (about 8 to 14).

```powershell
node scripts/scan.js --file=research/queries/<file>.txt [--start=N] [--max=250] --csv=research/scans/scan-<name>.csv --out=research/queries/gated-<name>.txt --band=evri5 --max-listings=600 --min-results=5
node scripts/gate.js --file=research/queries/gated-<name>.txt --band=evri5 --sample=6 --csv=research/gates/gate-<name>.csv --out=research/queries/passes-<name>.txt
node scripts/import-gate.js --csv=research/gates/gate-<name>.csv --shelf=research/queries/queries-<source>-map.csv --shelf-products=research/shelf/<shelf>.csv --date=<today> --term=<trade|general> --label="<Care & DIY|General>"
```

Re-judging a scan after a rule change costs no calls: `scan.js --from-csv=<same csv> --csv=<same csv> --out=...`. Same for a gate: `gate.js --from-snapshot=.gate/<file>.json`.

## Rules settled this week

- **Cut before the gate only for what the gate cannot see:** account risk and sizing. Everything else is judged by the market.
- **Account risk (scan holds back):** fragrance (by word, and by fragrance house when sold by the ml or when no shop name is attached), alcohol, gas canisters, nicotine, pharmacy medicines. Also reject by hand: licensed football merchandise, slimming medicines, intimate medical products.
- **Sizing:** clothing and footwear cut; aids are never cut for size (diabetic slippers, compression stockings, supports).
- **UK sellers only** in scan and gate (`itemLocationCountry:GB`). Proven: one "market" was 40 overseas listings.
- **One query per product:** model number, then brand plus product, then product alone.
- **Shop price is a lead, not a cost,** unless the match is exact (same barcode, set number or model, and pack). Match line on every card.
- **A model number named as something an accessory fits** is not the same product.
- **Small proved markets are set aside,** not refused. Early sales (3 or more in under a week) prove entry.
- **Nothing under £2 is thrown out;** it reaches her set aside with its figure. Busy-but-cheap items (£5 to £9 with many sales) are bundle candidates.
- **Import route:** stock bought abroad in small quantity, held and posted by her, labelled "Import, from CN" when the recorded supplier ships from outside the UK. UK sourcing stays the default.
- **Scripts never change her work:** decided, noted or sourced cards are left alone by imports, removals and decisions.

## The own-store idea (27 September)

The pipeline is built around eBay's limits. Her own store has none of them, and should run on a different rule: **breadth, good pages and being found on Google.** A store of 20 to 300 products reads as thin; 500 to 1,000+ across real sections gives shoppers something to browse and gives search engines pages to index.

The route to that size is **UK dropship supplier feeds**, not hand-listing shop finds:

- On her own store, a wholesaler shipping to her buyer is fine (the eBay restriction is eBay's).
- A feed brings licensed photos and descriptions, live stock and prices. Photos and copy from Lakeland, Hannworld and the rest are theirs and cannot be reused, and copied or thin pages rank poorly.
- Candidates from the sourcing list: Avasam (one account, many suppliers), Geko, UKSM Trade, TB Trade. Each needs an account and three answers: UK stock, plain packaging, a product feed.
- The research pipeline still earns its place: the eBay gate picks the products worth promoting and listing on eBay as well, and the shelves show what sells in each section.

To decide: which suppliers to apply to first, how the store imports and syncs a feed, and how sections and product pages are written so each one is worth indexing.
