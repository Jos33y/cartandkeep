Turn 2 · hero directions · none depends on photography you do not have

Three heroes. Structure, colour, or price.

Each one is a different answer to the same problem: a general store with strong words, weak supplier imagery and a catalogue on its way to a thousand lines. 2a makes the shop's structure the hero. 2b makes colour the hero. 2c makes the price the hero. Desktop first, then the same direction at 375, because that is where the orders will come from.

Six hues at roughly equal spacing, all at one chroma. Departments take a tone step inside their group, so twelve or eighteen departments still read as six places. Terracotta stays out of the set entirely, so it keeps meaning act and nothing else. One measured rule that comes out of building this: any field carrying text below 16px uses the deep tone at 0.48, not the mid tone. White small text on 0.55 lands between 3.0 and 4.0 to one and fails AA on every hue. On 0.48 it clears.
2a
The Index

The shop's structure is the hero. Argos front-of-catalogue logic: no imagery at all, just the whole shop laid out as a numbered index with counts and a price from. It is the only direction that gets better as the catalogue grows to a thousand, and the only one that is honest about having 78 lines today.

Why it works on a phone
The index is a list, and a list is what a phone is good at. No carousel to swipe, no image to wait for, the whole shop reachable inside one thumb-scroll. Price from does the merchandising that photography cannot.
Motion
Rows stagger in at 45ms each. The colour bar draws from zero height. On tap the row's colour floods the page as the department loads, so the transition and the wayfinding are the same gesture.
2b
The Colour Shelf

Flying Tiger and Monoprix energy. Six colour fields standing side by side like a shelf wall, group name set in wide Archivo, count underneath. The loudest of the three and the most alive. Panels widen on hover to show their departments, and the whole thing is built from flat colour, so it costs nothing to render and dates slowly.

Why it works on a phone
Six large colour targets, all well over 44px, all reachable without precision. This is the direction that feels most like walking into a shop, and the one that survives being viewed at arm's length on a bus.
Motion
Panels rise in sequence on load, wide type expanding from 88% to 104% width axis as each lands. Tapping one expands it to fill and carries its colour into the department page. This is where expressive earns its keep.
The risk
Loud enough that it needs the rest of the shop to hold the line. If the PLP and PDP stay quiet it reads as one great screen followed by a different website.
2c
The Price Board

Market stall and print catalogue. The price is the largest thing on the screen at 46px, the image is demoted to a small square, and the whole hero is a board of what is in the shop today. It solves the imagery problem by refusing to depend on it, and it puts the actual proposition, honest prices on dull useful things, at the top of the page.

Why it works on a phone
A price list is the most phone-native layout there is. Rows, no cropping, no swipe, and the number readable without looking closely. It also fills honestly at 78 lines and at 1000.
Motion
Prices count up from zero on first paint, once. Rows stagger. The board reshuffles with a FLIP transition when sorted, so you watch the shop reorder itself rather than blink.
The risk
It leads on price, and these prices are cost-plus-margin oddities. The direction needs the rounding conversation settled first or it puts a spotlight on the weakest data in the shop.
If you want my pick
2b for the homepage, 2a as the department hero.
They are not really competing. The Colour Shelf answers "where am I and what is here", which is the homepage job. The Index answers "show me all of it", which is what a department page and the all-departments page need. 2c is the most distinctive of the three and the one I would come back to once prices are rounded, as a recurring band further down the homepage rather than the hero itself.
